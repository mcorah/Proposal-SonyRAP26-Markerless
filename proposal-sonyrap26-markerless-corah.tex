\section{Abstract}

% concise summary of objectives methodology, and expected outcomes

Technologies such as Gaussian Splatting promise a future where
large static and dynamic scenes can routinely be captured and reconstructed for
photorealistic rendering or for analysis of biomechanics and motion.
Yet, today's motion capture systems require extensive camera arrays and
infrastructure to cover limited volumes.
Instead, we propose methods for low-cost, high-capture-volume
markerless motion capture based on coordinating fewer articulated cameras
to densely cover relevant regions of dynamic scenes.
Our methods will enable tracking and capture of multiple subjects moving
about a
large space such as a soccer field or a running track.
We will leverage methods we have developed for coordinating
multiple cameras to observe dynamic scenes---methods that enable coverage of
groups of subjects as they move together or spread apart or reorganize over the
duration of a task.
We propose combining these methods of camera coordination  with methods for
kinematic and dense reconstruction.
Dense and kinematic reconstruction methods developed through this work will be
evaluated via laboratory and field experiments and with
comparison to static and marker-based camera systems.
We plan to release the components of this system as open-source software
and to develop a %motion capture
dataset based on this work and will publish results in premier robotics
conferences.

\section{Introduction}

Existing markerless motion capture systems rely on arrays of static cameras
to reconstruct human motion in a designated capture volume.
Capturing motion of groups of people or athletes \emph{in situ} (e.g. during a
sporting event) as they move about in a large space (such as a football field)
becomes prohibitively challenging---particularly for dense capture---due to
increased requirements for numbers of cameras and amounts of infrastructure to
cover the large capture volume.
To address this challenge, we propose integrating methods for autonomous
coordination of mulitiple cameras for visual coverage of groups of moving
subjects with methods for reconstruction and markerless motion capture.
The proposed system, illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:markerless_system}, will
enable inexpensive markerless motion capture in large spaces
by coordinating multiple pan-tilt-zoom (PTZ) cameras to film and reconstruct
groups of moving subjects.

\subsection{Background}

Over the past six years, markerless motion capture has transitioned from
research topic to product, and products have emerged from leading manufacturers
of marker-based systems, e.g. Vicon, Optitrack, and Qualisys.
% Could add more detail here on biomechanics data collection
These systems promise applications from walk-in virtual reality
worlds\footnote{%
  Vicon demo ``Clockwork Forest'' presented at SIGGRAPH'23:
  \url{https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Ex4XwBqvjA}
}
to collecting biomechanics data for medical evaluations with minimal
time and preparation~\citep{wade2022peerj}.
Yet, like their marker-based cousins, these systems consist of large numbers of
cameras (e.g. 8+) that must be carefully located, mounted to tripods or a
truss system, and rigorously calibrated.
The goal of this project is then to \textbf{prototype and spur
development of a new class of low-cost and unobtrusive markerless motion capture
systems that can be readily deployed to cover large open spaces such as sports
facilities and arenas.}

The research community meanwhile has sought to cast away the anchor of
fixed infrastructure by developing markerless motion capture capabilities around
formations of UAVs~\citep{saini2019iccv,ho2021iros,hong2024eccv,saini2022ral}.
While these aerial systems would not promise accuracy comparable to systems
built around fixed cameras, they promise the ability to reconstruct human motion
outdoors and largely without bounds.
However, these systems have other limitations: research on
aerial markerless motion capture has focused on reconstructing
motions of individual people and neglects to address reconstruction of motions
of groups.
Further, because these approaches generally rely on robots that fly in a
circular formation around one subject, capturing multiple subjects, especially
if those subjects may sometimes move apart, poses a significant challenge
regarding view planning and coordination.

Alternatively, systems based on static PTZ
cameras~\citep{bachmann20193dv,yamamoto2022visigrapp}
could strike a balance and provide benefits of camera coordination
(e.g. operation in large and outdoor spaces) without limitations of aerial
systems.

similarly limited capture of individual subjects.
\emph{Therefore, our work will apply methods for camera coordination for visual
  coverage of multiple moving subjects that we have
  proposed~\citep{hughes2024cdc,suresh2024iros,bouy2026}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:multi-robot-videography}) and will integrate with development of
methods for markerless motion capture.}

\subsection{Impacts}

We expect our work to lead to development and proliferation of
low-cost, low-infrastructure markerless motion capture with widespread
impacts spanning sports, entertainment, and movement sciences.
These systems would make \emph{in situ} markerless motion capture viable in
domains and regimes where doing so would otherwise be challenging or infeasible
due to cost and the scale of the capture volume
\emph{transforming the economics of motion capture across scales and domains}.
For \textbf{kinematic motion capture}, the systems we propose could enable
motion capture at the scale of a sporting field with small numbers of
cameras (e.g. 4--6) and could become accessible to amateur and semi-professional
athletes and researchers.
Regarding \textbf{dense motion capture}, capture of teams sports at
the scale of a stadium may yet require tens or hundreds of cameras---but,
improving targeting will enable capture at higher resolution with less cameras
and infrastructure.

For \textbf{team sports}, motion capture data may augment practice film and
improve coaching and analysis and is already being used to automate refereeing.
\micah{Was there another paper I remembered on injury detection?}
Motion capture is also highly relevant to
\textbf{movement sciences}~\citep{wade2022peerj,nakano2020} and can lead
to improvements in rehabilitation and injury prevention.
For \textbf{entertainment purposes}, dense reconstruction of sporting events
and performances could enable synthesis of novel views and video streams or
enable new AR and VR applications.
Regarding \textbf{animation}, larger volumes and reduced costs and
infrastructure may increase the scope and scale of use of motion capture data.
Finally, proposed methods will enable development of human motion capture
datasets for \textbf{training machine learning models} that feature greater
diversity of movement, interactions between multiple subjects, and minimally
invasive methods; such datasets could improve humanoid robot motion control or
models for digital humans.

We consider challenges related to \emph{camera coordination} and

\paragraph{Camera coordination challenges}

\begin{enumerate}[resume,itemsep=0.3ex,topsep=0.3ex]
  \item Distributing camera views over subjects and obtaining observations from
    multiple perspectives
  \item Maintaining coverage of multiple subjects and groups that spread out and
    rearrange
  \item Achieving adequate coverage and resolution for kinematic or dense
    reconstruction
\end{enumerate}
The requirements for capturing motions of multiple subjects such as athletes
suggest developing methods suitable to coordinate cameras to observe all
subjects at once and from multiple perspectives; and these requirements for
maintaining visual coverage will be particularly intense for dense
reconstruction.
For example, the capacity to control camera zoom to increase resolution
over the surface of the subjects and scene becomes
paramount for downstream applications, e.g. entertainment technologies involving
synthesis of novel views or video.

\paragraph{Scene reconstruction and application domain challenges}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.3ex,topsep=0.3ex]
  \item Scaling of infrastructure requirements with capture volume
  \item Identifying and tracking subjects and forecasting motion
  \item Uncertainty in intrinsics and extrinsics
  \item Modeling of camera system and reconstruction process
\end{enumerate}

capture systems rely on fixed camera configurations to produce extensive
coverage over a capture volume---while fixed configurations may be

intensification of infrastructure and support structure produces secondary

leads to new technical challenges.
Identifying subjects and tracking and forecasting locations becomes critical
to the goal of coordination as pictured in Fig.~\ref{fig:markerless_system}.
Likewise, actuating camera orientation and zoom while simultaneously collecting
data to reconstruct subjects' motions undermines requirements for precise
extrinsic and intrinsic calibration.
This will necessitate development of motion capture methods that adapt
calibration continuously via feedback from encoders,
fine-tuning via image registration, or adopting methods amenable to uncalibrated
systems~\citep{bachmann20193dv,xu2022bmvc}.

Application to motion capture, especially dense reconstruction, will also
require awareness and modeling of nuances of the camera system and
reconstruction process.
Affects of motion blur, focus, exposure, and rolling (or global) shutter as well
as requirements for overlapping views must be accounted for in both coordination
and reconstruction.

\subsection{Gap: Robotic markerless motion capture}

\paragraph{Aerial motion capture}

\micah{could better address Michael black works with more nuanced person models}

Although motion capture systems are typically
built around static cameras, there has been interest in
markerless capture based on robotic and aerial
cameras~\citep{ho2021iros,saini2022ral,saini2019iccv,hong2024eccv,zhou2018icra}.
These methods demonstrate feasibility of reconstructing human skeletal motion
via camera-equipped aerial vehicles and enable motion capture in outdoor spaces
with loose bounds on the subject's motion but yet are subject to significant
technical and operational limitations:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.3ex,topsep=0.3ex]
  \item Limited number of tracked subjects
  \item Limited coordination of camera motion and zoom
  \item Operational constraints: battery life, safety, and policy requirements
\end{enumerate}

The referenced works on aerial motion capture all focus on reconstructing motion
of a single subject---in each case camera coordination or control is centered
on and relative to the subject's position often via a circular formation
consisting of 3--4 aerial vehicles.
These systems could hypothetically be adapted to capture multiple subjects such
as by fitting a circle around the group and constructing a formation around the
center.
However, if subjects spread apart, formation schemes would eventually fail.
Thus, these prior works do not address challenges related to
coordinating multiple cameras to cover and reconstruct subjects that spread
apart or form and reform groups.
These works also do not address control of camera intrinsics such as zoom, even
when using gimbal cameras with zoom functionality.

Further, while the challenge of aerial motion capture is compelling,
aerial platforms do not necessarily provide a desirable format for most motion
capture applications.
Limits on battery life lead to constraints on operation time or else
logistical challenges, and
operators must address safety concerns for subjects being filmed as well as
pursuant policy constraints.
Development of a practical and economical class of motion capture systems that
provides similar affordances---operation in large and outdoor volumes with
minimal infrastructure---is desirable.

\subsection{Gap: Camera coordination}

Camera coordination for multi-perspective visual coverage has not been
studied extensively.
Both aerial and PTZ motion capture methods adopt
formulations that center a single
subject~\citep{ho2021iros,saini2022ral,saini2019iccv,hong2024eccv,zhou2018icra,bachmann20193dv,yamamoto2022visigrapp}.
On the other hand, methods for multi-robot active perception or information
gathering~\citep{singh2009,corah2019auro,atanasov2015icra} address theoretic and
practical challenges related to coordinating multiple sensors or cameras
\emph{but not often for multi-perspective visual coverage};
rather existing works emphasize problems such as
environment monitoring~\citep{singh2009},
localizing targets~\cite{atanasov2015icra}, or
mapping 3D environments~\citep{corah2019auro}.

\subsection{Contributions}

Our recent contributions develop methods for coordination of multiple cameras
suitable for visual coverage
tasks~\citep{hughes2024cdc,suresh2024iros,bouy2026}.
The contribution of proposed work will be to
\textbf{1)} apply these advances in camera coordination to markerless
motion capture while accounting for domain challenges;
\textbf{2)} develop and evaluate methods for \emph{dense} and
\emph{kinematic} reconstruction of motions of multiple subjects \emph{in situ}
in large-scale environments; and
\textbf{3)} produce dataset and open source software releases.

\section{Methods}

We propose developing a prototype markerless motion capture system based on a small number
of PTZ cameras (e.g. 4--8) that will autonomously track and observe
multiple subjects moving together and separately either indoor in a laboratory
environment or in a large outdoor space such as a football field or running
track.
Our approach will build on methods of assignment-free view
planning that we have developed for multi-camera, multi-subject settings
based on submodular optimization
methods~\citep{suresh2024iros,hughes2024cdc,bouy2026}
while also developing and evaluating methods for \emph{dense} and
\emph{kinematic} reconstruction based on the data produced by that coordinated
camera system.

\subsection{Multi-camera coordination}

Our prior works on autonomous camera view planning optimize and maximize pixel
densities over subjects' surfaces for multiple cameras
jointly and consider application both with aerial
robots~\citep{suresh2024iros,hughes2024cdc} and PTZ
cameras~\cite{bouy2026}.
This approach applies submodular optimization methods to maximize view quality
directly without relying on fixed assignments and guaranteeing constant-factor
suboptimality for approximate solutions to the joint multi-camera optimization
problem.
Simulation results involving visual coverage of multiple moving subjects have
demonstrated emergent assignment and formation behaviors and superior
performance compared to formation methods when subjects split and rejoin
(Fig.~\ref{fig:multi-robot-videography}).
These methods are also suitable for nuanced camera models and modeling
occlusion~\citep{suresh2024iros}.
Our latest work involving PTZ cameras~\citep{bouy2026} relies on a pinhole
camera model with varying focal length; for application to motion capture, we
plan to incorporate more comprehensive camera modeling (considering, e.g.
focus, exposure, and motion blur) and considerations for
reconstruction (e.g. requirements for overlapping observations).

\subsection{Reconstruction}

Given a camera controller that autonomously obtains views of subjects
from multiple perspectives, we turn to development and integration of
methods for kinematic and dense reconstruction.

\paragraph{Kinematic reconstruction}
We plan to adopt similar methods for skeletal
reconstruction and markerless motion capture as have been demonstrated
previously for systems consisting of multiple PTZ
cameras~\citep{bachmann20193dv} or
UAVs~\citep{saini2019iccv,ho2021iros,hong2024eccv,saini2022ral}.
Our contributions in this area will \emph{focus on mitigating challenges related
to motion both of cameras (extrinsic and intrinsic uncertainty)
and of subjects (motion blur, limits of prediction accuracy).}
and will adapt and contribute to open-source software.\footnote{%
  FreeMoCap: \url{https://freemocap.org/}
}

\paragraph{Dense reconstruction}

\begin{development}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Greater senstivity to resolution and modeling of surface coverage
    \item Confront and model challenges such as necessity for overlapping views
      and challenges for finding corespondences, sparsity of views
    \item Multi-perspective, specular, and reflection
  \end{itemize}
\end{development}

Production of dense reconstructions from PTZ data will heighten
challenges related to appearance, calibration uncertainty, correspondences,
multi-perspective appearance (specular, reflection), and visual coverage.
This part of the proposed work will focus on the challenge of
obtaining dense reconstructions from sparse views and for modeling requirements
to obtain reliable reconstruction of complex scenes from coordinated cameras.
We will apply state-of-art methods for dense reconstruction via Gaussian
splatting~\citep{fei20243d,wu20253dgut,kerbl20233d} and will adapt to obtain
reconstruction from monocular PTZ data; we will adopt a co-design approach by
considering requirements for reliable reconstruction in the placement and
coordination of robotic cameras.

\begin{development}
We expect our activities would focus on developing systems for dense
reconstruction and addressing subsequent challenges for planning and autonomy.
From a systems standpoint, achieving dense reconstruction with PTZ cameras
may leverage models for metric depth estimation~\citep{ke2024cvpr}
but will likely still involve challenging registration and optimization
problems.
However, developing such capabilities would enable integration with
state-of-art methods for novel view synthesis with dynamic scenes such as based
on Gaussian splatting~\citep{zheng2025cvpr}.
Moreover, we expect dense, dynamic reconstruction to exacerbate challenges
related to \emph{resolution} and \emph{coverage}.
At the same time, a strength of our approach is that because we model and
optimize pixel densities over the surface of the scene directly, our methods are
therefore suitable to incorporating these twin challenges into our design and
modeling.
In this sense, coverage and resolution are requirements---we can adapt our
planning approach accordingly (utility design and problem formulation) and
design camera systems (numbers, placements, and models of cameras) to meet
requirements for dense reconstruction.
\end{development}

\paragraph{Dense capture system (capital equpment)}

The budget includes funds to develop a laboratory system for
validation and evaluation of dense motion capture methods, the \emph{dense
capture system}.
This system will consist of cameras and sensors to be installed in our motion
capture space (Fig.~\ref{subfig:motion_capture}).
This may include RGB cameras, multi-plane lidar, and additional PTZ units.
These cameras and sensors will be used to obtain baseline results for geometry
and appearance, and
this data may also serve to provide oracle depth estimates while prototyping
dense reconstruction methods.

\section{Expected output \& future direction}

The specific outcomes of proposed work will include:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.3ex,topsep=0.3ex]
  \item Publication of approach and results in premier robotics
    conferences (IROS and ICRA)
  \item Demonstration of PTZ markerless motion
    capture system capable of autonomously tracking and reconstructing multiple
    moving subjects and
    laboratory and field evaluation of this system
  \item Open source release of \textbf{software for camera coordination} for
    markerless motion capture
  \item Open source release of tools for markerless capture with PTZ data
    for kinematic reconstruction
    \textbf{(Relevance: biomechanics)} and dense Gaussian
    splatting reconstruction
    \textbf{(Relevance: media and entertainment technologies)}
  \item Dataset release for dense reconstruction with baseline and PTZ
    camera data
\end{itemize}
\emph{Proposed work will have transformative impacts spanning from
biomechanics and sports sciences to media and sports entertainment and beyond}
and will spur  development of low-cost, high-capture-volume markerless motion
capture systems.
By addressing both \textbf{kinematic} and \textbf{dense} reconstruction this
work initiates two parallel threads of technological and scientific inquiry that
will continue past the end of proposed work---within this research lab, via
collaboration, and by enabling follow-on work in the broader research community.
The development of this technology will reduce barriers to dense and skeletal
motion capture data collection for sports, dance, and various performances.
This in turn will enable collection of novel data, will expand the scope of
scientific study and enable development of media technologies based on dense
capture reconstruction of sporting events.

PI Corah is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the Colorado
School of Mines and director of the
Navigation, Aerial-robots, and Perception Planning Lab.\footnote{%
  NAPPLab: \url{https://www.napplab.org/}
}
He has extensive experience in the area of active perception and especially
regarding optimization for coordination of multiple
cameras~\citep{corah2019auro,corah2018cdc,corah2021iros}.
Recently, PI Corah has become interested in applying such methods to tasks that
involve camera coordination for multi-perspective visual coverage of groups of
moving subjects (athletes,
performers)~\citep{suresh2024iros,hughes2024cdc,bouy2026}.
These contributions form the basis for this proposal.

PI Corah is also interested in the relationship between representations of
three-dimensional environments and autonomous coordination of camera motions to
produce those representations, especially via the process of robot exploration
or autonomous
mapping~\citep{corah2019ral,corah2021icra,corah2019auro,goel2019fsr,kim2023ral}.
In particular, this interest led to contributions toward Gaussian mixture
mapping~\citep{corah2019ral} preceding the advent and popularity of Gaussian
splatting methods for reconstruction.
More recently, PI Corah was awarded a GPU hardware grant\footnote{
  An unrestricted gift including two RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUS.
} via the NVIDIA Academic Grant Program to support
research involving learning policies for autonomous
inspection and reconstruction; \emph{this GPU hardware will be available to
support proposed activities involving training Gaussian splatting models.}

PI Corah also has a background in development and deployment of mobile robot
systems.
As a postdoctoral researcher at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, PI Corah competed
with team CoSTAR in the final stage of the DARPA Subterranean
Challenge~\citep{morrell2024addendum}.
This competition involved field deployment of multi-robot teams to search and
explore underground environments representative of caves, mines, and urban
underground.
PI Corah's contribution to team CoSTAR built on prior experience
with field and laboratory deployments of aerial active perception
systems~\citep{corah2019auro,goel2019fsr}, and
PI Corah will draw on this background while leading proposed laboratory and
field deployments.
